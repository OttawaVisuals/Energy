What's on the rebate-eligible heat pump list?

The federal National Heat Pump Rebate pays by product: a heat pump qualifies if it is on the government's eligible list. This page looks at that list — how big the units are, how well they hold up in the cold, who makes them, and what kinds of systems are on it.

The program, in brief
Federal program page dated 2026-11-05
Payment$2,000 per household; up to $10,000 if the household is below the median income for its province or territory and size.
What qualifiesA heat pump on the eligible list, bought from and installed by an HVAC contractor, at a home you own in Canada. One rebate per household. No energy audit required.
DatesInstallations count from 2026-11-01. Applications expected to open in early 2027. Program expected to close in March 2034.
Size$2 billion, aimed at up to 820,000 households.

The program says its parameters are still being finalized and no payments are made until it launches. Nothing on this page is advice about which product to buy, and appearing on the list is not a performance endorsement.

Cold-weather performance: efficiency vs capacity kept

Two ways a heat pump can be good in the cold: it keeps most of its heating capacity at 5 °F (right), and it is efficient while doing so (up). Each dot is a distinct combination of ratings; bigger dots are shared by more certified products.

COP at 5 °F vs capacity maintenance

Capacity maintenance = heating capacity at 5 °F ÷ capacity at 47 °F: the share of its mild-weather output a unit still delivers in cold weather (0.80 means 80%). Lab-rated, not measured in homes. How complete is this?

ENERGY STAR only certifies some products. Where a listed product is not in ENERGY STAR's data, we show no rating for it rather than guess.

Ground-source (geothermal) heat pumps are on the list too but are not in the air-source ENERGY STAR data, so they are counted separately and not charted. Active/Archived is the status NRCan shows on its list; whether archived products remain eligible is not stated on the program page.

How this works, in plain language

To get the federal heat pump rebate, the unit you install has to be on an official list kept by Natural Resources Canada. Each product on the list is identified by an AHRI number, a reference number the industry uses for a certified combination of outdoor and indoor equipment.

The list says what a product is, but not how well it performs. For that we look the same AHRI number up in ENERGY STAR's public product data, which republishes the certified size and efficiency figures. Where the number is found, the product is counted in the charts. Where it is not, it stays in the counts of what is on the list but has no rating.

The figures are laboratory ratings from the certification program, not measurements in real homes. This page describes the list; it does not tell you which heat pump to buy or what a rebate will pay for you.

Advanced methodology — sources, join & caveats

Sources and vintage

Eligible list: NRCan Searchable Product List, product filter HP_NHPR, "Download all (CSV)", saved 2026-11-10. Fields: outside model, brand, inside model, furnace model, AHRI reference number, product group (ccASHP or GSHP), system type, status (Active/Archived). One row per model combination: 179,620 rows for 178,954 distinct AHRI numbers. No brand, group or status conflicts were found across rows sharing an AHRI number. Ratings: ENERGY STAR Certified Air-Source Heat Pumps (Socrata dataset w7cv-9xjt), CSV export saved 2026-11-10, 284,449 rows.

Join

Key: AHRI reference number, normalised to a string with any trailing .0 removed. List rows are collapsed to one per AHRI number; ENERGY STAR rows likewise (preferring continuously variable over two-stage over single-stage when several exist; no AHRI number had disagreeing 5 °F capacity or COP across rows). 12 ENERGY STAR rows had a blank AHRI number and were excluded and counted. List entries with no ENERGY STAR match stay in the counts with no rating.

Fields used

Heating capacity at 47 °F (Heating Capacity at 47°F (Btu/h)), COP at 5 °F (COP at 5°F), HSPF2 (Btu/Wh), Compressor Staging, Cold Climate, Markets. Ratings that are blank are counted as missing per field and left out of that chart (see the footnote counts in the JSON: dist_*.missing). Bins are fixed and open-ended at both ends.

Buckets

Heating capacity at 47 °F: 3,000 BTU/h wide from 9,000 to 63,000, open at both ends. COP at 5 °F: 0.1 wide from 1.7 to 2.5, open at both ends; a bucket labelled "1.8" holds 1.8 up to under 1.9. HSPF2: 0.5 wide from 8 to 12, open at both ends (0.25-wide buckets left alternating empty bars because ratings are reported at whole and half numbers).

Cold-weather scatter

Capacity maintenance = Heating Capacity at 5°F ÷ Heating Capacity at 47°F, the same metric as the heat pump tool's tiers. Rows with the same capacity at 47 °F, capacity maintenance (rounded to 0.001), COP at 5 °F and compressor type are drawn as one dot sized by the number of AHRI numbers sharing it (3,789 distinct dots across all matched units). Screening follows the heat pump tool: capacity maintenance above 1.30 or COP above 3.0 is excluded and counted on the page; units missing either capacity are counted, not plotted. Compressor type colours the dots. No tiers are drawn, because the COP axis piles up at the 1.80 reporting floor and cannot be split into thirds (see HeatPump/METHODOLOGY.md).

Brand measure

Distinct outdoor-model strings per brand among Active air-source entries, brand names upper-cased and trimmed. Related brands that sell the same outdoor model are not merged. It is a count of product lines on the list, not of units sold.

Measured vs modelled vs assumed

Measured: none of this is field measurement. Rated: capacity, COP, HSPF2 (certification-test values). Reported: ENERGY STAR's copy of those values. Our earlier check against the AHRI directory found 5 °F capacities agree to within 2% on 3,447 models present in both. Assumed: nothing is modelled on this page.

Limits

What the data can't tell us: how many units are installed or sold, which products homeowners choose, or real-world efficiency. What we assumed: that ENERGY STAR's republished figures match the AHRI certificate (verified on a sample, not all rows). What would change the answer: an updated NRCan list (it changes as products are added or archived), or a rebate rule that excludes archived products, which would make the "Active" view the right one.

The ENERGY STAR-matched units are the ENERGY STAR-certified subset of the list: 95.5% of Active air-source entries but only 18.2% of Archived ones, so the "Include archived" view is weighted toward what ENERGY STAR still certifies. The comparison line (all ENERGY STAR units sold in Canada) includes non-cold-climate models, which is part of why rebate-eligible units show higher HSPF2.

Not used: the AHRI Directory itself. Its terms bar reuse of public-site data and it now blocks automated access, so no AHRI data is scraped. Pipeline: HeatPump/pipeline/build_nhpr_eligible.py. Full notes: NEWHEATPUMPS.md ↗.
