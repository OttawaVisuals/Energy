01 — THE PROVINCES

A provincial scorecard

The same eight numbers for every province, so the national averages don't hide the enormous spread between them. Click a column to sort.

By the numbers: heat-pump adoption ranges from 50.3% of matched retrofits in New Brunswick and 45.3% in Quebec down to 3.4% in Alberta and 2.0% in Saskatchewan, with Ontario at 14.5%. 03 — WHAT WORKS

What makes a retrofit succeed

Built on the 1,326,144 pairs that logged at least one real measure — the zero-measure re-audits are set aside. The pattern is blunt: mechanical change beats shell tweaks, and depth compounds.

By the numbers: among homes that did exactly one measure, a heat pump returned a 25.5% median saving (141,677 homes) and a heating-system change 20.1%, against 7.9% for air sealing alone, 6.6% for roof insulation and 6.1% for windows. Median saving rises with each added measure, from 16.2% at one to 68.9% at all eight.

04 — WHO PARTICIPATES

Who takes part

Postal areas sorted into five equal groups by neighbourhood median household income, lowest to highest.

By the numbers: participation rises with neighbourhood income, from 11.6% in the lowest quintile to 16.4% in the highest. Heat-pump rate runs the other way (34.7% down to 19.0%), and median saving is close to flat (22.1% to 19.8%). These are area averages; see the caveat below before reading them as household behaviour.

By neighbourhood income quintile

Q1 = lowest-income neighbourhoods, Q5 = highest. Participation, heat-pump rate and typical saving.
These are area averages, not household measurements. A quintile describes the postal area a home sits in, not the income of the household that retrofitted it. The lower-income quintiles here contain a higher share of Atlantic and Quebec postal areas, which also have the highest heat-pump rates province-wide, so the income gradient and the provincial gradient cannot be separated in this data. Read the chart as a description of areas; it does not support a claim about what lower-income households do.

07 — THE TIMELINE

Twenty years of programs

Matched audits per year — the initial (pre-retrofit) audit and the completed (post-retrofit) audit that closes each pair. The two big waves map onto the two big federal programs.

08 — OPEN QUESTIONS

Questions for the EnerGuide team

Things this dataset cannot answer on its own. Each one is a limit we hit while building the page, written down rather than papered over with an assumption.

  1. Which FSA vintage do audit records carry? Participation divides ~20 years of audits by a single 2021 census snapshot. If a record stores the postal code as captured at audit time, then areas Canada Post later redrew accumulate audits their present-day dwelling count cannot carry — we measured this and suppressed 80 postal areas because of it, plus 62 more whose FSA code has no 2021 census record at all. Confirming the vintage would tell us whether these can be recovered by remapping rather than dropped.
  2. Is there a participation denominator you would prefer? Audited homes ÷ census dwellings counts condo units that never receive a house audit, which pushes downtown high-rise areas near zero. A house-only or eligible-stock denominator would make participation comparable between urban cores and suburbs. Does one exist?
  3. What explains the provincial spread in heat-pump adoption? Adoption runs from 50.3% of matched retrofits in New Brunswick to 2.0% in Saskatchewan. The pattern is stable and reappears inside every climate and income breakdown we tried, but this data contains no programme, rebate or contractor-availability variable, so we can describe the spread and not attribute it. Is there a rebate-history dataset that could be joined?
  4. Does climate add anything beyond province? Grouping homes by heating-degree-days produced a heat-pump gradient that turned out to track provincial composition rather than temperature, so we removed the section rather than publish a climate finding we could not separate from province. Is there a within-province comparison you would consider sound?
  5. Is earliest-D-to-latest-E the right way to pair a re-audited home? Changed 2026-07-24: homes with more than one initial or follow-up audit (149,145 nationally, ~9% of homes holding both) used to be excluded entirely; they are now reduced to their earliest initial audit and latest follow-up, which lifted every figure on this page to a 1,451,433-pair base from 1,369,305. About 55% of those homes have two of each, so their single row may span two separate retrofit projects. We chose this because it captures the home's whole change rather than an arbitrary half, and because the headline results barely moved when the homes were added — but if the intended reading is one row per project, pairing each initial audit with the next follow-up would be the correct treatment instead. Which matches how EnerGuide records these?
  6. How should zero-measure pairs be treated? 125,289 matched pairs (8.6%) record no tracked upgrade between the two audits, with a median saving of 3.2%. We report them as a count and exclude them from every "what worked" statistic. Are these re-audits, data-entry gaps, or measures outside the eight tracked flags?
  7. Are the opportunity-score weights defensible? The 0.30 / 0.25 / 0.20 / 0.25 split across pre-EUI, pre-GHG, pre-1980 share and low participation is our judgement, not an estimate from data. The ranking moves if the weights move. If the programme has a view on relative priority, we would rather encode yours.
