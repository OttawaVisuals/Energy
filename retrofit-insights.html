02 — THE PROVINCES

A provincial scorecard

The same eight numbers for every province, so the national averages don't hide the enormous spread between them. Click a column to sort.

04 — WHAT WORKS

What makes a retrofit succeed

Built on the 1,251,023 pairs that logged at least one real measure — the zero-measure re-audits are set aside. The pattern is blunt: mechanical change beats shell tweaks, and depth compounds.

So what: depth beats breadth — a heat pump alone saves ~25%, windows alone ~6%, and each added measure lifts the median another 5–10 points. The same effort spent on a heat pump saves roughly 4× what windows-only saves.

05 — CLIMATE

The climate lens

Homes grouped by their postal area's heating-degree-days (how cold the winter is). It looks like a climate effect — but read the caveat.

So what: what looks like a climate effect is mostly a provincial one — the mid-cold heat-pump spike is Quebec's rebate boom, not colder winters. Saving % barely moves across climate bands.

Heat-pump adoption & savings by winter severity

Grouped by heating degree-days (HDD, base 18 °C). Colder → right.
Composition, not causation: the 4,500–5,500 HDD band is dominated by Quebec (aggressive heat-pump rebates); the 5,500–6,500 band is the prairies (little uptake). The bump tracks province, not temperature — don't read it as "colder → more heat pumps".

CDD · the cooling angle

The same homes grouped by cooling degree-days (CDD, base 18 °C). Hotter summers → right.
High-CDD is southern-Ontario gas country (low heat-pump uptake); the gradient again tracks provincial fuel mix, not cooling need.
06 — WHO PARTICIPATES

Who takes part

Postal areas sorted into five equal groups by neighbourhood median household income, lowest to highest.

So what: cheaper neighbourhoods show more heat pumps — but that's Atlantic Canada and Quebec in the data, not lower-income households. Participation rises weakly with income; the deeper savings sit in the lower-income (Atlantic/Quebec) quintiles.

By neighbourhood income quintile

Q1 = lowest-income neighbourhoods, Q5 = highest. Participation, heat-pump rate and typical saving.
Ecological fallacy warning: a neighbourhood's income quintile describes the area, not the individual participants. The "lower-income → more heat pumps" pattern is entirely provincial composition — the cheap-housing quintiles are Atlantic/Quebec-heavy, regions with electric/oil heat and aggressive provincial rebates. It is not evidence that poorer households buy more heat pumps.

By dwelling value quintile

The secondary axis: postal areas grouped by median dwelling value instead of income.
Same mechanism as income — the lowest-value quintile (Q1) shows the highest heat-pump rate because it is Atlantic/Quebec-heavy, not because cheaper homes are greener.

09 — THE TIMELINE

Twenty years of programs

Matched audits per year — the initial (pre-retrofit) audit and the completed (post-retrofit) audit that closes each pair. The two big waves map onto the two big federal programs.

So what: the 2009 peak is the ecoENERGY Retrofit era; the 2023–24 peak is the Canada Greener Homes Grant. The post-2024 fall-off — after the grant closed to new applicants — is already visible.
