                The day falls into place once you see the squeeze: 20 hours of appliances, but only 19 hours outside the peak. The slow cooker, at 1 A, takes the one on-peak hour, and dinner cooks without the oven.

                At night the order matters. The house is still busy at 9 pm, so the laundry goes first and the EV charges from 1 to 7 am. EV first would have hit 58 A.

                That's load management: the same appliances on the same day, with a lower peak. It keeps the 100 A panel and keeps the big loads off the most expensive hours.

                Line 5 complete · Energy and GHG savings

                3.8 t CO₂e a year

                4,110 kg (furnace) − 340 kg (heat pump) ≈ 3,770 kg
                Energy bought: 19,400 → 4,870 kWh, 14,530 kWh less

                The loop moves heat instead of making it. Outdoors, the cold refrigerant is colder than −2 °C air, so it soaks up heat and boils. The compressor squeezes that gas until it's hotter than the house, the indoor coil hands the heat over, and the expansion valve drops the pressure to start again.

                That's why almost two-thirds of the heat comes free from outside. On Ontario's mostly nuclear and hydro grid, each kWh carries about 70 g of CO₂e, against roughly 210 g for a kWh of propane.

                3.8 tonnes a year is about 80% of what a typical car emits in a year. Switching the water heater and stove off propane too saves about another 0.5 t.
